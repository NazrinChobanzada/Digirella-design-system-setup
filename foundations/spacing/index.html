Guidelines

Eight rules

The numbers only work if they follow these. Each one appears, in some wording, in most of the fourteen systems surveyed.

Inside is never larger than outside

Space inside a group is always smaller than the space around it. That relationship is what tells someone where one object ends and the next begins — get it backwards and a row of cards reads as one block. Card padding is 16, so the gap between cards is at least 16.

Three zones, three ranges

2–12px inside a component, 8–24px between components, 24–96px for layout. A value that crosses zones is usually a mistake: 32px inside a card is a layout decision that wandered in.

Parents own spacing

Use gap on the flex or grid parent, and padding on containers. Margins on children collapse, double up against each other, and leave the last item with trailing space nobody asked for. The one exception in this system is the field label, which carries a padding-bottom so that label-to-field reaches 8 while the rest of the stack sits at 4.

Height first, padding follows

Fix the control height, set the horizontal padding, and let the height do the vertical work: (height − line-height) ÷ 2 − border. Every control in this system takes padding: 0 <x> plus a height token for exactly this reason — vertical padding and a fixed height fight each other, and the height should win.

Off-grid values are for optics only

--space-optical-6 and --space-optical-10 exist to compensate for icon padding and borders. A 36px icon-only button holding a 16px icon needs 10px, and no amount of wishing makes that 8 or 12. They are named as optical so nobody reaches for them to space a section.

The icon side takes less padding

An icon carries its own whitespace, so the side with an icon gets less padding than the side with text. A tag is 12 on the label side and 8 on the icon side; without the asymmetry the icon looks pushed away from its own label.

Tokens keep their value; the choice moves

A token's value does not change with the screen size — you pick a different step instead. --page-margin and --section-gap are the only exceptions in this system, and they move one step at a time: 16 → 24 → 32 → 40.

Density is one step, not a second component

Compact mode steps every value down one token; comfortable steps up. There is no separate compact card. Set data-density on an ancestor and the same components retune themselves.

Text rhythm

A heading belongs to the text below it, not the text above it. Two to three times as much space above a heading as below is what makes that legible without a single rule or divider — measured against this scale, a section break at 32 over a 12px heading gap is 2.7×, and 48 over 12 is 4×.

One constraint worth knowing before you move a surface token: a field sitting on --surface-page rather than on a card puts --border-control at exactly 3.00:1 — it meets 1.4.11 with nothing to spare. Darkening the page surface or lightening the control boundary breaks it.

Compact never shrinks a hit area below --touch-target-min. If a row has to get smaller than 44px on touch, extend the hit area with transparent padding rather than making the target smaller than a finger.

Known divergences

Two places where this system deliberately does not match the consensus. Both are recorded here so they read as decisions rather than as drift.

Control heights are 30 / 38 / 46, not 32 / 40 / 48

Thirteen of the fourteen systems surveyed settle on 32 / 40 / 48. Digirella sits 2px under at every step, and the reason is the field.

The 38px field is a density decision: at 38 with 14px text a six-field form fits without folding, where 44/16 did not. The consensus rule that matters more than the exact number is that a field and a default button must share a height, so the button was pulled to 38 rather than the field pushed to 40. A field and a button now sit on one line with no adjustment, which is what the rule was protecting.

The ends are then set 8px either side of the default, so the ladder steps evenly: 30, 38, 46. An even ladder is worth more than matching the consensus number, because it is the thing a designer reads off when choosing a size.

One consequence to know: the large button at 46px is the only control that clears 44px, so it is the only one that meets the touch target on its own. Everything else needs its hit area extended.

Semantic tokens

tokens/spacing-components.css is the component layer: 126 tokens that name a relationship rather than a value, so a component can be retuned without touching the scale underneath it. Product code and components read these; the --space-* scale is for layout.

The naming follows the architecture: --<component>-<relationship>, and every one of them resolves to a step on the scale. None holds a raw pixel except the four that hold a height — --button-min-width, --menu-item-height, --tooltip-max-width and --avatar-stack-overlap — which are sizes, not spacing.
