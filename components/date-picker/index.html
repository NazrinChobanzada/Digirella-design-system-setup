DatePicker New

A field that collects a date, a time, or a span of them. The calendar is optional equipment — open it when the user needs to browse, not when they already know the answer.

Overview

A field that collects a date, a time, or a span of them. The calendar is optional equipment — it helps when the answer depends on where a date sits relative to other dates, and it slows people down when it doesn't.

The deciding question is never "do we need a calendar" — it's whether the user already knows the date. If they do, a calendar is in the way; if they don't, typing is.

Guidelines

Principles

Known dates are typed, not browsed

Nobody finds their own birthday faster in a calendar. If the user can answer from memory or from a document in front of them, give them fields to type into.

birth date, invoice date, expiry → type

Calendars are for relationships

Open a calendar when the answer depends on what day of the week it is, what else is booked, or how far away something is.

"first Monday after the 15th" → calendar

Typing is always allowed

Even with a calendar, the field accepts text. Power users type faster than they click, and a calendar-only field is unusable for keyboard-heavy workflows.

input + calendar, never calendar alone

Store ISO, show local

One canonical value in the data (2026-10-07), one readable format on screen, decided by the user's locale — never the developer's.

value 2026-10-07 · shown 07/10/2026

Choosing a variant

What you're asking forUseWhy
A date the user knows — birth date, invoice date, expiryDate fieldThree boxes to type in beat scrolling back 40 years
A date they have to work out — a booking, a deadline, a shiftDate pickerThe grid shows weekdays, weekends and what's nearby
A span — a stay, a report period, a leave requestRange pickerTwo months side by side so the span is visible in one gesture
A reporting window over finished daysRange with presetsNine times out of ten the answer is "last 7 days"
A time of dayTime pickerA list of plausible times, typing for anything else
A moment — a meeting, a publish time, a cut-offDate + timeTwo fields that share one value, plus the time zone
A period — a billing month, a quarter, a tax yearMonth or year pickerDays are noise when the unit is a month
An approximate date — "sometime in spring"Not a pickerA select or text field; a calendar forces a precision that doesn't exist

Variants

Date field

Three labelled boxes — day, month, year. No calendar. The fastest way to collect a date somebody already knows, and the only one that works when the date is decades away.

For example, 15 3 1990
Never auto-advanceJumping focus after two digits fights correction and breaks keyboard navigation
Accept month names"jan", "January", "01" and "1" are all the same month — removing a large share of all date errors
Hint with an example"For example, 31 3 1980" reads better than DD/MM/YYYY
Validate on submitNot per box: 31 is valid until the month says otherwise
One errorOne message for the whole group, with focus going to the first box in error

Date picker

A text field with a calendar attached. The field is the primary input; the calendar is the assist. Opening it never clears what's typed, and closing it never loses it.

The calendar button opens a picker.
OpeningThe calendar icon opens the picker. Focusing the field does not — people typing don't want a panel over the page
Opening monthThe month of the current value, or the minimum date, or today — in that order
SelectingWrites a formatted date into the field, closes the calendar, returns focus to the field
TodayMarked with a ring, not a fill — it is not a selection
ClosingSelection, Esc, outside click or tabbing out. Esc restores the value that was there before

Range picker

Two dates, one control. The in-between days are part of the answer, so they're drawn — and the second click is guided by a preview that follows the pointer.

7 nights · Calendar shows both months side by side on desktop.

Two monthsSide by side on desktop so a span crossing a month boundary is one gesture
OrderFirst click sets start and clears the end. Clicking before the start restarts the range rather than erroring
PreviewHovering after the first click shades the provisional span — the keyboard gets the same preview as focus moves
Length limitsA maximum span disables days beyond it as soon as the start is chosen — don't wait for submit

Range with presets

For dashboards and logs, where the honest default is "the last week" and the calendar is the exception. Presets are relative; the calendar is absolute; the control shows both.

1 Oct – 7 Oct 2026

The resolved dates always appear — "Last 7 days" is readable but not checkable on its own.

Relative vs absolute"Last 7 days" keeps moving; "1–7 October" doesn't. Store which kind was chosen, not just the resolved dates
Show the resolutionAlways display the actual dates a preset resolves to
Six at mostToday, Yesterday, Last 7 days, Last 30 days, This month, Custom
Custom lastAlways the final item, and selecting it keeps the current range as its starting point

Time picker

A list of plausible times plus a field that accepts anything. The list is a shortcut, not a constraint — 12:38 is a real time even when the list counts in halves.

Type "9", "9:15", "14" — or pick from the list.
Increments30 minutes by default, 15 for scheduling-heavy products. The list is suggestions; typing overrides it
RangeScope it: a shift planner starts at 06:00, not 00:00
12 or 24 hourFrom the locale, not from preference. AM/PM is never a subtle dropdown
Parsing"9" → 09:00, "930" → 09:30, "9p" → 21:00. Echo the normalised value back on blur
PairingNext to a date field, not inside it: two inputs, one value, one label above both

Date + time

Two fields that share one value. The pair is the component; the time zone is part of it, not a footnote.

UTC+4
One label"Publish at" above both, with each field labelled for assistive tech
DefaultsPicking a date with no time set fills a sensible default — 09:00, or the next half hour for "now"-like actions
ZoneShown beside the time, always. Editable when the audience is elsewhere; read-only when it isn't
Past timesIf the date is today, disable times that already passed and say why

Month & year picker

When the unit of the answer is a month, a quarter, or a year, days are noise. Same field, different grid.

Calendar shows a 3×4 month grid.
Grid3×4 months, with the year in the header and steppers either side
ValueStore the first day of the period and the period type — "October 2026", not "1 October"
QuartersFour cells, labelled with their months: "Q4 · Oct–Dec"
LimitsDisable months with no data instead of hiding them, so the gap is visible

Behaviour

Typing & parsing

Be strict about what you store and generous about what you accept. Every rejected input here is a user who typed a perfectly sensible date.

They typeAccept asNote
7/10/20262026-10-07Order follows the locale — this is why the format hint is not optional
07.10.26 · 07-10-20262026-10-07Accept the separators people actually use, including spaces
7 oct · 7 October2026-10-07Month names and abbreviations; current year implied
71020262026-10-07Digits with no separators, parsed by position
31/02/2026ErrorReal-date check: "31 February must be a real date"
9 · 930 · 9p09:00 · 09:30 · 21:00Time shorthand — echo the normalised value back on blur
Normalise on blur, not per keystrokeReformatting while someone is still typing moves the caret and eats characters. Wait for them to leave the field.
Never silently correctIf "31/02" becomes "28/02" without a word, the user submits a date they never chose. Reject it and say what's wrong.

Formats & locale

DecisionRule
Stored valueYYYY-MM-DD, or a full ISO timestamp when a time is involved. Never a locale string in the database
Displayed formatFrom the user's locale. 07/10/2026 and 10/07/2026 are the same day to a machine and different days to a person
AmbiguityWhere a mistake is expensive, write the month: "7 Oct 2026". It survives every locale
Format hintUnder the field, as an example: "For example, 31 3 1980" reads better than DD/MM/YYYY
Week startLocale-driven — Monday in most of Europe, Sunday in the US. Not a personal preference setting

Limits & disabled days

RuleDetail
Show, don't hideUnavailable days stay in the grid, visibly disabled. A missing day reads as a rendering bug
Say why"Fully booked", "Before your start date", "Weekends not available" — in the day's accessible name and in a legend when the pattern repeats
Clamp navigationDon't let someone page into a year where every day is disabled
Typed valuesThe same limits apply to typing. A disabled day typed by hand is still an error, with the same message

Time zones

Plain datesA birth date has no time zone. Store it as a date, never as a timestamp at midnight UTC — that's the classic shift to the day before.
MomentsA meeting is a timestamp. Store UTC, display in the viewer's zone, and show which zone is being displayed.
Ranges in analyticsBoundaries follow the user's zone, not the server's. State the convention near the control: "Times are UTC+4".
Daylight savingTwo hours a year don't exist and two happen twice. Time lists must not offer the missing one.

Accessibility

Rolesrole="dialog" on the calendar popup with aria-label="Choose date"; role="grid" on the calendar table; role="gridcell" on each day button.
Keyboard — calendarArrow keys move by day; PageUp/Down move by month; Ctrl+PageUp/Down move by year; Home/End jump to start/end of week; Enter or Space select; Esc closes and returns focus to the field.
Disabled daysUse aria-disabled="true", not the HTML disabled attribute — keyboard focus must still be able to move past them without getting trapped.
Todayaria-current="date" on today's cell. Not aria-selected — today is not the selection unless the user chose it.
Selected dayaria-selected="true" on the chosen day. The accessible name includes the full date: "15 November 2026".
Live regionThe month heading is in a live region so screen readers announce it when the calendar navigates between months.
Touch targetsEach day button is at least 32×32px. The calendar icon is 24×24px minimum with a visible label or aria-label.
