Components

Alert

New

Overview

A short message about the state of something, shown in the interface rather than in a dialog. It never blocks the page, always says what happened, and — when there is something to do — names the next step.

Listing published
Your product now appears in search.
Your shipping agreement renews on 12 September.
Stock is running low
Three products have fewer than two left. Review stock
Payment failed
Your saved card has expired. Update card

Principles

Put the message where the problem is

A field error belongs under the field, a section problem inside the section, a service-wide problem at the top of the page. Distance between the message and its cause is what makes people miss it.

Severity is meaning, not emphasis

Red means something is broken or blocked, not "this is important". A warning dressed as an error teaches people to ignore red.

Say what to do next

Every message that reports a problem names the action: retry, fix a field, contact someone, wait. A message with no next step is noise with an icon.

Never interrupt for something routine

Saves, copies and small confirmations are toasts at most. If the user must read it to continue, it isn't a toast — and if they must act on it, it isn't an alert, it's a dialog.

Placement

Same component, four positions. The position decides scope, lifetime, dismissal, and what live-region role it takes.

Inline — one control or one row

Sits immediately below the element it concerns. Never dismissible: it disappears when the condition does.

Enter a valid email address.
ScopeOne control or one small group
LifetimeWhile the condition holds; removed when it's fixed
DismissibleNo — dismissing would hide the reason the control is blocked
RoleTied to the field with aria-describedby; role="alert" only when it appears after an action

Section — one card or panel

Inside the container, under its heading. Scrolls with the content it belongs to.

Identity not verified
Payouts are held until your account is confirmed. Verify now

Panel content follows here.

ScopeEverything in that card, panel or step
LifetimeWhile the section is open and the condition holds
DismissibleOnly when advisory
PositionBelow the section heading, above the section content

Page — this screen

Top of the page content, under the page header. One at a time — if two conditions apply, write one message.

Toast — something that just happened

Floating, transient, non-blocking. Confirms an action the user took and then goes away. The only placement with a timer, and the only one that must survive being ignored.

Severity

Five severities, independent of placement. Colour, icon, and words all carry it — colour never alone.

Error — something failed or is blocked
Payment declined. Try another card.
Warning — nothing is broken yet, but there will be a consequence
Trial ends in 3 days. Upgrade to keep access.
Success — an action the user took completed
Invoice sent to buyer.
Info — context or a neutral change they didn't cause
Maintenance scheduled for Sunday 02:00–04:00.
SeverityMeansDismissible
ErrorSomething failed or is blocked. The user cannot continue as intendedNo, while the condition holds
WarningNothing is broken yet, but something will go wrong or has a consequenceUsually
SuccessAn action the user took completedYes, or auto as a toast
InfoContext or a neutral change of state they didn't causeYes

Severity rules

Error is for blocked, not for bad

If the user can still finish what they came to do, it is a warning. Red is a limited currency — spend it when something actually stopped.

Never colour alone

Icon shape and the first words carry the meaning too. "Error: payment declined" reads the same in greyscale and to anyone who can't separate your red from your green.

One severity per message

If a single alert has a failure and a tip in it, it is two messages. Split them, or drop the tip.

Success rarely needs to persist

A success that stays until dismissed turns into furniture. Prefer a toast; keep a success banner only when it's the proof of a transaction.

Anatomy

Icon
Severity-specific shape. Never the only signal — the title or message carries the meaning in words too.
2
Title
Optional but recommended above one line of body. Sentence case, no period, never just "Error".
3
Message
One or two sentences. What happened, then what to do.
4
Action
At most two. Never a filled primary button — it competes with the page's own primary.
5
Dismiss
Top-right, 24×24 target. Only on dismissible alerts — absent on errors that block action.

Actions

RuleDetail
At most twoOne primary, one secondary. A third belongs in the page, not the message
Quiet stylingLinks or outline buttons. A filled primary button inside an alert competes with the page's own primary action
Verb first"Retry payment", "Update card" — not "Click here" or "OK"
DestructiveNever perform destructive actions from an alert. Alerts can link to them; they can't execute them
Undo in toastA toast with Undo gets the longer timer and must not auto-dismiss while hovered or focused
Taking actionTaking the primary action dismisses the message. Dismiss alone never performs the action

Timing

Only toasts have a timer. A timer is an accessibility decision: anything that disappears on its own must be pausable, and anything carrying an action must not disappear while it's being used.

MessageDurationNote
Plain confirmation — "Saved"4–5sLong enough to read twice at a glance
Confirmation with detail6sTwo lines need more time than one
With an action — "Undo"8–10sThe action is the point; it must still be reachable when the eye arrives
Error or warningNo timerStays until dismissed, or until it stops being true
On hover or keyboard focusPausedResumes on leave. Without this, a keyboard user can never reach the Undo

Stacking

Toasts — 3 visibleNewest nearest the corner. Beyond three, queue the rest and show "+N more".
One page bannerCombine conditions into one message rather than stacking two.
Never mix placementsA failed save is a toast or an inline error — not both. Showing the same event twice trains users to ignore one of them.
Collapse repeats"3 uploads failed", not three identical toasts.
Page + inline is fineAllowed, because the scopes differ: the page problem at the top, the field that caused it marked below.

Accessibility

Rolesrole="status" for toasts and advisory alerts; role="alert" for danger alerts and error summaries that appear after an action.
Live regionsThe container must exist in the DOM before the message arrives — creating the container and the message together announces nothing. Keep it present and visually empty between messages.
Never colour aloneEvery severity carries a glyph and a word as well as a colour — the meaning survives greyscale and colour-blindness.
Focus — toastsNever steal focus. Toast actions are reachable by Tab while visible; the timer pauses the moment any element inside receives focus.
Focus — error summaryAfter a failed submit, move focus to the error summary. Its links move focus to each field in error.
On dismissReturn focus to the nearest sensible anchor — the heading above it, the next alert, or the main region. Never to body.
TimingAuto-dismissal pauses on hover and focus (WCAG 2.2.1). Anything the user must act on never auto-dismisses at all.
