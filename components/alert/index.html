Components

Alert New

Alerts communicate a persistent status or message that is directly relevant to the user's current context — they stay on the page until dismissed or resolved.

Overview

An alert is inline and persistent. It does not vanish on its own, and it does not interrupt the user's flow. It lives in the page, at the top of a section or at the top of the full content area, and tells the user something they need to know to make sense of what they are looking at — a service outage, a pending verification, a risk they may not have noticed.

Four variants cover the semantic range: info for neutral context, success for completion, warning for something that needs attention, and danger for something that requires immediate action or has already gone wrong.

ℹ
Verification pending
Your shop is visible but purchases are paused until your identity is verified. This usually takes 1–2 business days.
✓
Payment received
£73.00 was collected successfully. Funds will be transferred to your account in 2 business days.
⚠
Listing expires in 3 days
Renew "Handmade ceramic mug" to keep it visible in search results.
✕
Payment failed
Your saved card was declined. Update payment method to resume your subscription.

All four variants. The left border and icon carry the semantic meaning; colour reinforces it but does not carry it alone.

Guidelines

When to use

Alerts are for persistent, page-level or section-level status. They are not for every message — overusing them trains users to ignore them.

SituationRight patternWhy
A service degradation affecting the whole pageAlert, top of pageThe status is global, persistent, and needs to stay visible.
A form field has a validation errorInline field errorThe error belongs next to the field, not at the top of the page.
"Your profile is 80% complete"Alert, top of sectionRelevant to this section, persistent until resolved, no urgency.
"Order placed successfully"ToastTransient feedback for a completed action. Should disappear; does not need to stay visible.
A payment card about to expireAlert, warning variantThe risk is real but not yet catastrophic. Persistent — it should stay until the user acts.
A required action is blocking the userAlert, danger variantThe current state is broken. The user cannot proceed without fixing it.
⚠
Your card expires next month

Do use an alert for a persistent state the user needs to act on before their next session.
ℹ
Settings saved
Your notification preferences have been updated.
Don't use a persistent alert for a transient confirmation. A toast disappears on its own and does not require the user to dismiss it.

Variants

The variant is a semantic choice, not a visual one. Pick the one that accurately describes the situation — don't use warning for every non-critical message, or danger for anything you want to highlight.

VariantTokenUse whenDon't use for
Info --status-info Neutral context the user should know about but does not need to act on immediately. System notices, feature announcements, help text for a complex flow. Errors or anything requiring action. "Info" reads as low priority.
Success --status-success A process has completed and left a lasting positive state — verified identity, connected account, activated feature. Transient confirmations ("order placed") — use a toast. Anything negative.
Warning --status-warning Something is wrong or about to go wrong and the user can fix it. Expiring card, incomplete profile blocking a feature, a quota at 80%. Errors that have already occurred (those are danger). Anything you just want to emphasise.
Danger --status-danger Something has already gone wrong, or the user is blocked from proceeding. Payment failed, account suspended, a required field is missing in a way that breaks the whole flow. Warnings about future risk. Anything the user can safely ignore.

Content guidelines

Title

Optional, but recommended when the message has more than one sentence. The title summarises the state in five words or fewer: "Payment failed", "Account suspended", "Action required". It does not repeat the variant name ("Warning: …") — the colour and icon already say that.

Body

Always required. One or two sentences explaining what happened and, if the user can act, what to do. Avoid technical error codes in the body — log those separately. "Your card was declined by your bank" is useful; "Error 402: payment_failed" is not.

Action links

Optional. A single inline link or button that takes the user to the resolution. It should be specific — "Update payment method" rather than "Click here". The link colour matches the variant token so it reads as part of the alert, not a generic anchor. Don't add more than one action — if two things need doing, the alert body should explain the order and link to the first.

✕
Subscription paused
Your last payment failed. Update your card to resume access.
A specific action link names exactly what to do. The user knows what pressing it will do.
✕
Error
Something went wrong. Click here to fix it.
"Error" and "Click here" are both meaningless. The user is no closer to understanding the problem or solving it.

Anatomy

Anatomy

1 ⚠
2 Shop suspension warning
3 Your listing count has exceeded the free tier limit. Upgrade your plan to keep all listings active.
  1. Icon — 20px, colour-matched to the variant token. Always present. It reinforces the severity without relying on the subtle background tint alone. The icon is decorative (aria-hidden="true"); the variant is communicated through the role and text.
  2. Title — Optional. --font-size-300 at weight 600. Summarises the state. When omitted, the body carries the full message.
  3. Body — Always present. --text-secondary. The explanation and, where relevant, the consequence.
  4. Action — Optional. A single inline link or text button. Colour-matched to the variant. One action maximum.
  5. Dismiss button — Optional icon-only ghost button. Present on dismissible alerts; absent on persistent ones. See the Dismissible section for when to include it.

Placement

Where an alert sits determines who sees it and what they associate it with. The choice is about scope — who does this message apply to?

Top of pageFor messages that affect everything on the page — an outage, an account-level restriction, a global feature announcement. Placed between the navigation and the page title, so it appears before the user scans any content.
Top of sectionFor messages that affect only the content in a section or a specific form — a field-level conflict that surfaces after submission, an incomplete section in a multi-part form. Placed at the top of the relevant container, inside its padding.
InlineFor messages that relate to a single element — a table row whose data is stale, a card whose status needs attention. Placed directly inside or immediately adjacent to that element.
✕
Your account is suspended pending verification.
An account-level suspension alert at the top of the page, before the content it affects.
✕
Your account is suspended pending verification.
Don't bury a page-level alert in the middle of the content. The user may never reach it.

Behaviour

Dismissible vs persistent

Most alerts should be persistent — they stay until the condition they describe is resolved, not until the user presses a button to make them go away. A user who dismisses a "payment failed" alert has not fixed the payment; they have just hidden the warning.

When to allow dismissal

Allow dismissal when the alert is informational and the user may genuinely not need to act: a feature announcement, an upgrade prompt, a contextual tip. These are things the user might already know about, and forcing them to see it on every visit is more annoying than useful.

Do not allow dismissal on danger or warning alerts that block a workflow. If the account is suspended, dismissing the alert does not unsuspend it — and hiding the message creates a worse experience than leaving it visible.

ℹ
New: Bulk listing tools
Upload up to 50 listings at once from the Listings page.
Do allow dismissal on informational announcements. The user may already know about the feature.
✕
Payment failed
Your card was declined. Update your payment method to continue.
Don't make a blocking error dismissible. Hiding the alert does not resolve the payment failure.

Remembered dismissals

When a dismissible alert is closed, store the dismissed state in local storage or a user preference so it does not reappear on next visit. An announcement the user has already seen and dismissed should stay gone.

Accessibility

The key failure mode for alerts is the silent update — adding an alert to the DOM after page load without announcing it to assistive technologies. The criteria below address this and the supporting requirements.

Live regions

  • 4.1.3Alerts added dynamically must use a live region. Use role="alert" (implicit aria-live="assertive") for danger and warning; use role="status" (implicit aria-live="polite") for info and success. high risk
  • 4.1.3role="alert" announces immediately, interrupting whatever the screen reader is saying. Only use it when the message is genuinely urgent. Overuse of role="alert" causes announcement fatigue. high risk
  • 4.1.3Alerts present in the HTML on initial page load do not need a live region — they are read as part of the document structure.

Role and naming

  • 1.3.1The variant must not be communicated through colour alone. The icon and the text together convey the severity to users who cannot perceive colour.
  • 1.4.1Each variant has a distinct icon — info ℹ, success ✓, warning ⚠, danger ✕ — so the four states are distinguishable without colour.
  • 4.1.2The dismiss button has an aria-label that names its action in context: "Dismiss verification notice", not just "Close".

Focus and interaction

  • 2.1.1The dismiss button is keyboard-operable. A user who dismisses with Tab + Enter should not lose their place in the document.
  • 2.4.3After dismissal, focus moves to a logical nearby element — the next focusable item below the alert, or the element that was focused before the alert appeared.

What you still have to do

  • Add role="alert" or role="status" to dynamically injected alerts.
  • Give the dismiss button a descriptive aria-label that includes the subject of the alert.
  • Ensure the icon carries aria-hidden="true" — the variant is already communicated through role and text.
  • Store dismissals persistently so users do not see the same announcement on each visit.
